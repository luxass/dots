# Patterns and examples

Use these clues when prose feels inflated, repetitive, or hard to follow. An isolated
word or punctuation mark is not evidence of AI authorship.

## Empty claims

- Replace "a pivotal moment in an evolving landscape" with what changed and for whom.
- Replace "significantly improves performance" with a measured result, if one exists.
  Otherwise describe the change without inventing a measurement.
- Replace "experts believe" with the relevant named source when available. If evidence
  is missing, qualify the claim or flag it for the author.
- Remove generic conclusions such as "the future looks bright" unless they communicate
  an actual position the author intends to express.

## Formulaic phrasing

- Cut openings such as "Of course!" and "Great question!" when they add nothing.
- "In order to" usually becomes "to"; "due to the fact that" becomes "because".
- State the point directly when "not just X, but Y" adds no useful contrast.
- Do not force ideas into groups of three or a "from X to Y" range that has no scale.
- Keep a word such as "may" when the evidence is uncertain; cut stacks of qualifiers
  such as "could potentially possibly".

## Precision and voice

- Prefer "use" to "utilize". Keep a specialized term if it names the mechanism better.
- Replace vague metaphors with what happens: "the database stays close at hand" might
  become "the process opens a local SQLite file", if that is what the code does.
- Keep the same name for the same thing. Do not rotate "gate", "guard", and "budget
  check" when all three refer to one check.
- Name the actor when it helps: "the compiler validates queries". Passive voice is
  useful when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
- Do not add first-person opinions, staged informality, or intentional mistakes just
  to make the writing seem human.

## Formatting

Prefer sentence-case headings and minimal emphasis. Remove decorative emojis or
repetitive bold labels unless the requested format uses them purposefully. Keep lists
that genuinely help scanning. Preserve the author's typography when required by a
quotation, template, or publication style.
