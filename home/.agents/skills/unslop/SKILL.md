# Unslop

Make the writing sound like a person saying something specific.

Preserve facts, intent, uncertainty, citations, and the requested tone. Do not invent
opinions, anecdotes, measurements, or sources to make text feel more human. Preserve
quotations and code unless the user asks to change them.

- Lead with the point. Cut throat-clearing, generic praise, and empty conclusions.
- Name the actor, action, or mechanism. Use the real term consistently instead of
  cycling through synonyms or inventing metaphors.
- Prefer plain words and concrete claims. Keep technical terms when they are more
  precise for the audience.
- Split sentences that require backtracking, but keep useful conditions and context.
  Let sentence length follow the thought.
- Use only the headings, lists, and emphasis that help the reader.
- Avoid em dashes in ordinary prose as a house preference. Use natural punctuation;
  parentheses, colons, and semicolons are not inherently wrong.
- Remove redundant hedging, not genuine uncertainty or necessary qualifications.

For a dedicated editing pass or a stubborn passage, consult
[patterns and examples](references/patterns.md). These are editing clues, not banned
words or a test of who wrote the text.

When asked for a rewrite, provide the rewrite. Add an explanation only when requested
or when an edit would change meaning. Do not update this skill while editing other work.
