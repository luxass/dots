# Document modes

Use the reader's need to choose a structure. A document can contain different modes
in clearly separated sections when that saves the reader unnecessary navigation.
Split pages when their audiences or purposes conflict, not to satisfy a rigid rule.

## Tutorial: learn by doing

State what the learner will build and what they need beforehand. Provide a reliable
path with observable results at useful checkpoints. Include enough explanation to
orient them; link deeper background when it would interrupt the exercise.

## How-to: complete a task

Start with the goal and prerequisites. Give actionable steps, relevant branches, and
the expected outcome. Include warnings before risky actions. Keep background short,
but do not omit context needed to choose safely.

## Reference: look up a fact

Organize around the API, configuration, command, or other thing described. State
accepted values, defaults, limits, errors, and examples. Separate recommendations
from facts and identify uncertainty rather than hiding it.

## Explanation: understand a decision or mechanism

Start from a bounded question. Connect behavior to constraints, alternatives, and
tradeoffs. Distinguish documented rationale from your interpretation. Include a
concrete example when it makes the mechanism easier to understand.

## Short work artifacts

For an RFC, foreground the proposed decision, constraints, alternatives, and open
questions. For a PR description, explain the change and relevant verification.
For a commit message, summarize the coherent change and why it was needed. Follow
the repository's template where one exists.

The four documentation modes are adapted from Diátaxis. These are local application
guidelines, not a reproduction of the framework's full rules.
